PACE4 4.38.4: termination beyond rotational stability
9 November 2026

Problem and diagnosis

For 48Ca + 124Sn at Elab=300 MeV, FYRST=0, 10,000 cascades and seed
708849395, 4.38.3 reports 2,446 unchanged 172Yb residues. Every one starts
at J>=117 hbar and follows the artificial E2 yrast cascade. The original
PACE instructions explicitly describe this cascade as non-statistical;
it is a treatment of spin-inhibited decay, not a calculation of survival
of a rotationally unstable nucleus.

The change in visibility since 4.36 is caused by the corrected initial-spin
sampler in 4.37. The original loop draws a fresh random number for every
cumulative-probability bin, strongly biasing the result toward low spin.
The corrected single-draw sampler remains unchanged.

Physical interpretation and scope

A rotationally vanishing fission barrier marks the loss of the metastable
compound-nucleus configuration in the macroscopic barrier model. Such a
configuration cannot be treated as a bound residue merely because a level
density table supplies no available transitions. High-spin capture beyond
that limit belongs to the barrierless/fast-fission regime rather than the
ordinary compound-nucleus statistical cascade.

This release uses a terminal barrierless-fission approximation within
PACE's existing assumption that the sampled projectile+target system is
formed. It does not calculate formation competition, quasifission,
prescission emission, dissipation, a fast-fission timescale, or fragment
distributions. Its barrierless cross section is therefore a model-defined
endpoint contribution, not a validated dynamical prediction.

Implementation

Before statistical channel selection at each (Z,N,Ex,J), inspect the
already calculated barrier. Terminate as fission when the spin-dependent
barrier is exactly zero while the zero-spin barrier and barrier multiplier
are positive and finite. This guards against mistaking missing/invalid
barriers or a zero multiplier for rotational instability. A positive
barrier, even a small one, retains ordinary statistical competition.
An empty level-density table by itself never triggers this treatment.

The existing SIERK() wrapper is retained: in particular its ordinary
IS=0 branch uses the Cohen-Plasil-Swiatecki FISROT table. This change does
not replace that model with the full Sierk BARFIT fit. For 172Yb in this
calculation, the active table first gives zero barrier at J=103 hbar.
FYRST, level densities, sampled spins and input cross-section normalization
are unchanged. Barrierless events are counted in Total fission with a
separate reported subset; they are not discarded or renormalized away.

The binary traceback retains its existing format and fission mode 6.
Barrierless endpoints have fission probability 1 and no emitted particle
energy/angle. No synthetic statistical width is passed to the lifetime
calculation. Positive-barrier yrast traps retain the legacy gamma treatment.

Validation

The reported seed gives 0 unchanged 172Yb residues, 4,135 barrierless
events and 4,483 total fission events, with all 10,000 cascades accounted
for. Regression coverage compares individual initial spins with 4.38.3,
checks all high-spin traces terminate without artificial gamma emission,
tests J=102/103 boundary cases, FYRST=1 and output without traceback,
and compares even/odd low-spin and bound gamma-trap controls byte-for-byte.
